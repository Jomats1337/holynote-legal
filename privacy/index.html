Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: October 28, 2026.

This policy covers HolyNote on iOS and Android. Your personal library stays on your device unless you request online processing, such as creating an AI note or Scripture study. Separate technical, subscription, and measurement information is processed as described below; the integrations differ between platforms.

Important privacy summary
  • HolyNote currently works without a user account or cloud note sync.
  • Your saved notes, recordings, images, chat history, reading preferences, and Today progress are stored locally on your device.
  • Audio, reviewed Scripture text, YouTube links, and questions are sent only when you request an online AI feature.
  • Religious reflections, prayers, and sermons can reveal sensitive beliefs. Please share only content you are comfortable processing online.
  • On iOS, Amplitude measures technical onboarding and product interactions using a random app-installation identifier. Adapty forwards configured subscription lifecycle events to Amplitude. Your answers, Scripture, notes, prayers, audio, and AI request content are not sent to Amplitude.
  • iOS versions with the native Meta integration send installation, activation, technical app/device information, and available automatically detected purchase information to Meta for measurement. The Meta app-instance identifier is also associated with your Adapty profile. Adapty IP-address collection is enabled when this native integration is configured.
  • iOS versions with Jomlink, our first-party HolyAttribution service, send installation identifiers, device/network context, and available AppsFlyer and Adapty identifiers to our server. Jomlink can compare app launches with visits to our link pages. Accepted server records are kept for 96 hours, with linked observations expiring no later than their parent record; see section 9.
  • The Android app sends installation, session, and selected commercial interaction events to Meta, and Adapty shares subscription lifecycle events with Meta. These integrations use pseudonymous identifiers, not the content of your library or AI requests.
  • Android also uses AppsFlyer for install and session attribution. Its app-instance identifier and limited campaign information are linked to your Adapty profile; IP addresses may be used to infer approximate location.
  • Android crash reporting through Firebase Crashlytics is optional and controlled by the separate “Crash reports” setting.
  • We do not sell personal data or show third-party advertising.

2. Information stored on your device

HolyNote stores the following information locally so the app can work:

  • notes, folders, transcripts, generated summaries, Scripture studies, flashcards, and reflections;
  • recorded or imported audio and any images you attach to a note;
  • Bible chat history, highlights, personal verse notes, reading position, and display preferences;
  • Today completion, streak progress, onboarding choices, and app settings;
  • technical installation/session identifiers, delivery receipts, and temporary retry queues used by the measurement integrations described below.

This local information is not automatically synced to a HolyNote account. You can delete notes and conversations inside the app. Removing the app or erasing its data generally removes local information. On iOS, copies may remain in device backups controlled by you or Apple. Android app backup and device transfer are disabled. Deleting local data does not automatically delete records held by our service providers.

5. AI processing and sensitive content

HolyNote uses OpenAI API services to transcribe audio and generate requested notes, studies, and answers. We configure supported Responses API requests with storage disabled. OpenAI states that API inputs and outputs are not used to train its models by default unless the API customer opts in. OpenAI may retain API content in abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days unless a different approved retention control applies or longer retention is legally required. See OpenAI’s API privacy information.

Sermons, prayer requests, religious beliefs, names, voices, and personal stories can be sensitive. Avoid including information you do not have the right to share. AI output may be inaccurate or incomplete; verify Scripture wording and important conclusions before relying on or sharing them.

7. Analytics, attribution, and tracking

HolyNote does not show third-party ads or sell personal data. We use the measurement integrations described here to understand acquisition, product use, subscriptions, and advertising performance. The prepared iOS configuration does not collect Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA) and does not display an App Tracking Transparency (ATT) prompt. This describes those technical controls; it is not a claim that every use of other identifiers or device/network information falls outside Apple's definition of tracking. A privacy policy or the absence of IDFA does not replace any required permission.

iOS native Meta measurement: in versions containing the configured integration, Meta starts with the app and automatically handles its installation/activation and available purchase detection. It may receive product and transaction identifiers, amount, currency, purchase timing, and trial/offer information available to the SDK. Detection of every trial, renewal, or other subscription event is not guaranteed. HolyNote passes the Meta SDK's pseudonymous app-instance identifier to Adapty so that the profile can be associated with that identifier. Our configuration disables IDFA collection, automatic screen metadata, codeless debugging, and product-improvement data collection, and limits Meta event-data usage. It does not provide a separate in-app Meta opt-in or measurement switch. These restrictions do not make the remaining information anonymous or stop the receiving server from seeing connection information.

iOS first-party Jomlink measurement: versions containing HolyAttribution start it independently of note creation, onboarding, and purchases. It can collect a random installation identifier, event/session identifiers, first-SDK-open and delivery times, launch/session metadata, app version/build, Apple vendor identifier (IDFV), device model and operating-system version, language/locale, time zone and clock format, screen size/scale/brightness/orientation, and available App Store storefront information. It can also record limited availability diagnostics and heuristic indications of VPN use or device modification. These indications are not proof of a person's identity, VPN provider, or device security status. Our server sees the IP address of the HTTPS connection; the app's additional public-IP and AppTransaction probes are disabled in this integration.

Jomlink also receives genuine AppsFlyer UID and Adapty profile identifiers when available, limited attribution outcomes and campaign/ad-set/ad/source fields from provider callbacks, and allowlisted acquisition-link context such as a click identifier or source/referrer information. It does not receive full private note links or arbitrary URL query strings. Provider identifiers are comparison keys, not proof that two records identify the same person. An existing user's first launch of this SDK is not necessarily a new App Store installation. There is currently no separate in-app Jomlink opt-in or measurement switch.

Jomlink link pages: pages running our web measurement code can send selected campaign and click parameters supplied in the link (including platform click identifiers), user-agent and available browser/platform information, language, time zone, screen dimensions/scale, and referrer origin. The web SDK can use browser local storage for its delivery queue; link pages may retain a receipt for handing a click identifier to the app. Clearing browser storage does not delete records already accepted by our server. This is separate from our static legal/support pages, which do not run this measurement code.

Jomlink's current diagnostic comparison can use timing, connection IP, language, time zone, and screen context to suggest a possible connection between a link visit and an app record. Such suggestions are heuristic, can be ambiguous or wrong, and are not verified personal identities or calibrated probabilities. Disclosure of this processing does not by itself establish that a particular matching use is permitted by platform rules or applicable law.

iOS product analytics: versions including Amplitude start technical product measurement with the app. This helps us understand where onboarding stops, whether features complete successfully, and how to improve the product. The app does not provide a separate Amplitude opt-in or analytics switch. Onboarding answers and user-generated content are excluded. Session replay, automatic screen, element and network capture, remote automatic-capture configuration, and SDK diagnostics are disabled. The Amplitude SDK does not populate IP address, IDFV, carrier or geographic-location event properties in our configuration; as with other online services, a receiving server still sees connection information. Data is processed using our US Amplitude project. Subscription lifecycle events, such as trial start, conversion, renewal and cancellation, are forwarded by Adapty rather than duplicated as client financial events.

Android app measurement: the current Android build initializes the Meta SDK when the app enters the foreground. It reports installation and app activation, plus a limited set of commercial events such as onboarding completion, paywall views, plan selection, checkout outcomes, and restore outcomes. These client events do not contain your answers, Scripture, notes, prayers, or AI content. The current build has no in-app advertising-measurement switch and does not wait for a separate advertising opt-in.

Android acquisition attribution: AppsFlyer starts with the application and measures installations and sessions. HolyNote passes its pseudonymous app-instance identifier and a limited set of available campaign, ad-set, ad, source, and attribution-time fields to Adapty after the subscription profile is available. Android ID, IMEI, App Set ID, and advertising-identifier collection are disabled. This does not prevent the receiving provider from processing the IP address or deriving approximate location from it. The Android client sends no custom interaction or financial events to AppsFlyer; subscription lifecycle delivery to Meta continues through Adapty. AppsFlyer measurement does not depend on the separate optional crash-report setting.

Subscription measurement: the Android app passes the Facebook SDK’s “anonymous” app-device identifier to Adapty after SDK activation. Adapty can then associate subscription events with that identifier and forward configured trial, purchase, conversion, renewal, cancellation, expiry, billing, and refund events to Meta. This identifier is pseudonymous: its name does not mean that the related data cannot be associated with an app installation or combined with other information by a provider. Financial lifecycle events are forwarded by Adapty rather than duplicated by the Android client.

Android collection of the Google Advertising ID is disabled, and the app does not request the Advertising ID permission. Meta automatic purchase logging, codeless events, and advanced matching are disabled in the app. These restrictions do not eliminate the separate app-instance identifiers or event sharing described above.

Optional Android diagnostics: Firebase Crashlytics remains disabled until you enable “Crash reports” in HolyNote’s Settings. You can turn it off there. This preference is independent of Meta measurement; turning it off does not disable subscription verification or Meta events.

8. Subscriptions and purchases

Apple processes App Store payments on iOS, and Google Play processes purchases on Android. The relevant store manages your subscription account. We do not receive your full payment-card number. We and Adapty may receive purchase identifiers, product, price, currency, transaction timing, renewal, trial, refund, and subscription-status information so we can provide paid access, restore purchases, prevent fraud, and understand subscription performance. On Android, configured lifecycle and purchase information, including transaction identifiers, product, amount, and currency where applicable, is also shared with Meta for subscription and advertising measurement.

On iOS, versions with native Meta automatic purchase detection can also send available purchase metadata to Meta as described in section 7. The existing configured subscription-measurement path through Adapty and AppsFlyer is separate. Jomlink's installation/provider-identifier observations are not a second purchase-reporting channel.

9. Retention and deletion

  • Local content: remains on your device until you delete it, erase app data, or remove it through device management or backup controls.
  • Temporary media: is normally deleted from HolyNote’s processing server when the job succeeds, fails, or is cancelled. A system crash may delay cleanup until operational maintenance.
  • AI job transcripts and results: remain in the current server’s temporary in-memory job store for no more than 24 hours by default, or until the service restarts.
  • Jomlink server records: accepted click, first-SDK-open, provider-identifier, context and delivery records are retained for 96 hours from receipt by our server. Linked observations expire no later than their parent first-SDK-open record and can therefore be removed sooner. Automatic cleanup runs on requests, at service startup, and approximately once per minute while the service is running; maintenance or an outage may delay cleanup until the service resumes. This is separate from the AI job store's 24-hour retention period. Uninstalling HolyNote, deleting a note, or clearing browser storage does not itself delete these records. Temporary migration backups are checked approximately once per minute and removed when the oldest record in that backup reaches its 96-hour deadline; backups do not intentionally extend this retention period.
  • Jomlink local delivery state: the app and web SDK use temporary retry queues with a 24-hour delivery window, separate from the server's retained records. Local installation identifiers, accepted receipts, and any page-specific handoff state may remain until the relevant app or browser data is removed. A retry window does not set a deletion deadline for information already stored on our server.
  • Provider data: is retained under the relevant provider’s policy, including OpenAI’s default abuse-monitoring period described above and Apple, Google Play, Adapty, AppsFlyer, Amplitude, Meta, or Firebase records used for purchases, product analytics, attribution, diagnostics, fraud prevention, and legal compliance. Retention varies by provider and purpose; uninstalling HolyNote does not automatically erase these records.
  • Security logs: are kept only as long as reasonably necessary for security, reliability, abuse prevention, dispute handling, and legal obligations.

HolyNote currently has no user account, so we may need limited additional information to locate a server job, installation, or provider profile. For a Jomlink access or deletion request, contact support@holynote.app and provide only a relevant record/installation identifier if you already have it, or the approximate date and context of the activity. Do not email sermon audio, prayer text, full payment details, or other sensitive content. We may need to verify a request; a date or shared IP address alone does not prove ownership of a record. We assess applicable rights and any legal or security retention obligations when handling requests, including relevant backup copies. There is no in-app Jomlink server-deletion control at present.
